It's a Bird?

With over 400 species of birds seen in Missouri it can be hard to tell one bird apart from another. So why not start by focusing on the black and white birds and learning about them.

Rose-breasted Grosbeak

Most common in hardwood forests in the northern half of Missouri. Both sexes sing, but the male sings much louder and clearer. Has a rich, robin-like song. Common name “Grosbeak” refers to its large bill, which is used to crush seeds. The rose breast patch varies in size and shape in each male. Males arriving in small groups first in spring are joined by females several days later.

Red-headed Woodpecker

One of the few woodpecker species in which male and female appear the same. The bill is not as well adapted for excavating holes as in other woodpeckers, so it chooses dead or rotten tree branches for nests. Later nesting than the closely related Red-bellied Woodpecker and will often take over its nesting cavity. Prefers more open or edge woodland with many dead trees.

Downy Woodpecker

One of the most abundant and widespread woodpeckers in Missouri, found throughout where trees are present. Stiff tail feathers help brace this bird like a tripod as it clings to a tree. Like all woodpeckers, it has a long barbed tongue for pulling insects from tiny places. Both sexes drum on branches or hollow logs to announce territories that are rarely larger than 5 acres.
